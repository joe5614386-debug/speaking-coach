    How scoring works: speech recognition listens (On-device: an open-source Whisper model running in this browser, so your voice never leaves your computer; Browser: Google’s or Microsoft’s online service), then the text is checked phrase by phrase. Accuracy: a phrase is green once you said it in order in any attempt, so restarting or correcting yourself is fine. Fillers (uh, um), repeated words and “I mean” are ignored. Yellow words were heard only in pieces (half credit); red words were missed. Fluency is scored separately and goes down with restarts, fillers, repeats and a slow pace. You get 15 seconds per sentence (longer for a whole passage); listening stops once every phrase is green, or after a few seconds of silence. It measures whether a listener would understand you, not individual sounds. Your recordings (“My recording”, “Model, then me”, “Intonation”) stay in this browser tab and are never uploaded; they disappear when you close the tab. Sound check (beta, optional) listens to each sound with an open-source phoneme model (wav2vec2 by Facebook AI, Apache-2.0, about 240 MB, downloaded once) running on this computer. Unlike word recognition, it does not guess words or sentences, so it can notice a wrong sound (think → sink) even when the word itself was recognised. Expected sounds come from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary. Only the main words are checked, and normal variation (weak vowels, fast speech, accents) is ignored; an echoey room or a noisy mic can still cause a wrong hint now and then.
